# RAG inside larger apps: Onyx, AnythingLLM, Dify, Open WebUI

> Snapshot: 2026-10-17. Short profiles of popular applications where RAG is one feature among several.

| Project | Licence | Activity | Retrieval | Footprint | Lesson |
|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| [Onyx](https://github.com/onyx-dot-app/onyx) (formerly Danswer), about 32.5k stars | **Split:** MIT community edition; everything under `ee/` is proprietary, including permission sync for about 20 connectors ([licence](https://github.com/onyx-dot-app/onyx/blob/main/LICENSE)) | Very active (v4.9.2, 2026-10-15) | OpenSearch (Vespa removed in September 2026); default embedder nomic-embed-text-v1 | API server, workers, two model servers, PostgreSQL, OpenSearch, Redis, object storage; at least 4 vCPU and 10 GB of RAM, 16 GB recommended ([docs](https://docs.onyx.app/deployment/getting_started/resourcing)) | Connectors and a **permission-aware index**, with access fields stored on chunks at index time, are its moat. That is the model for our multi-user extension |
| [AnythingLLM](https://github.com/Mintplex-Labs/anything-llm), about 67.3k stars | MIT | Active (v1.18.0, 2026-10-13) | **Vector search only** (top 4, threshold 0.25), an English MiniLM embedder by default, an optional native reranker whose own code notes about 5 s to rerank 20 documents on a laptop CPU ([source](https://github.com/Mintplex-Labs/anything-llm/blob/master/server/utils/vectorDbProviders/lance/index.js)) | Desktop app with embedded LanceDB; multi-user only in Docker | Its zero-infrastructure installer explains its popularity, and we should match it. Its retrieval defaults are exactly the gaps we target. Cap reranking candidates on CPU |
| [Dify](https://github.com/langgenius/dify), about 158.7k stars | **Modified Apache-2.0, not OSI:** a commercial licence is needed to run a multi-tenant service; branding must stay ([licence](https://github.com/langgenius/dify/blob/main/LICENSE)) | Very active | A visual, plugin-based ingestion pipeline (since 1.9); vector, full-text or hybrid retrieval with a weighted score or a reranker; parent-child chunks; a summary index | PostgreSQL and Weaviate by default; at least 2 CPUs and 4 GB | Ingestion as a pipeline of typed steps is the right abstraction. The licence rules out reusing code, and RAG is one feature of a general app platform |
| [Open WebUI](https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui), about 156k stars | **Open WebUI License, not OSI:** BSD-3 plus a branding clause for deployments above 50 users, and a CLA ([licence](https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui/blob/main/LICENSE)) | Very active | English MiniLM embedder; hybrid search **off by default**; when on and the vector store lacks native hybrid search, it builds BM25 **in memory at query time** over the whole collection, tokenised by splitting on whitespace ([source](https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui/blob/main/backend/open_webui/retrieval/utils.py)) | Chroma by default | RAG bolted onto a chat UI ends up with weak defaults. Possible integration: open-rag-engine as an external retrieval backend for Open WebUI (FR-API-08) |

## What this tells us

- **The biggest audiences use the weakest retrieval.** The two most-starred chat apps default to an English-only embedder, vector-only or whitespace-tokenised search and no evaluation.
- **Licences in this space are drifting away from open source:** Dify and Open WebUI are not OSI-approved, and Onyx keeps permission sync in proprietary code. An AGPL engine with access control in its open core would stand apart.
- **Installation friction decides adoption.** AnythingLLM's popularity comes from its one-click install, not its retrieval quality. QR-USE-01 sets our bar.
